## v0.1 foundation

## Question

Which guarantees and failure modes of bisection, Newton, and secant iteration remain visible when
the methods share the same binary64 arithmetic, tolerances, and iteration budget?

## Result

The seven prespecified runs behave as expected. Bisection solves the sign-changing cubic bracket
within its interval-halving bound and correctly declines the same-sign bracket around an
even-multiplicity root. Newton converges rapidly near the selected simple cubic root, converges more
slowly for a repeated root, and enters the exact prespecified two-cycle from `x₀=0` for
`x³−2x+2`. Secant solves the cosine fixed-point equation without a derivative and refuses an update
when its two starting function values are equal.

[`reports/v0.1-root-finding.json`](../reports/v0.1-root-finding.json). That file is generated by the
same Rust library exported to WebAssembly.

## Interpretation

The comparison shows why method names alone are insufficient. Bisection's slower progress buys a
geometric certificate when its assumptions hold. Newton's local speed does not remove dependence on
the derivative and starting point. Secant's derivative-free update still needs distinct function
values. These are established properties made inspectable, not novel discoveries.

## Limits

The suites are intentionally small and adversarial, use analytic functions and derivatives, and do
not sample real scientific workloads. Timing is excluded because browser and hardware effects would
confound these educational cases. Production-library equivalence, multiple roots beyond the
explicit rejected-bracket example, noisy evaluations, automatic differentiation, arbitrary
expressions, and interval arithmetic remain outside the evidence.
